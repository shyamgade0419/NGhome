| UI | Shared skeleton, empty-state, table and form primitives; resident home re-prioritisation | Runs alongside each phase, applied to the screens it touches. |

### Decisions that affect production and are therefore recorded here

1. **New role `SECURITY_GUARD`.** An additive Postgres enum value (`ALTER TYPE … ADD VALUE`); existing rows are untouched. Guards get gate endpoints only.
2. **No camera in v1.** Scanning QR codes requires the Android camera permission, which the app deliberately blocks and which the published privacy policy and Play data-safety form state is not used. Phase A therefore uses short typed codes. QR scanning needs a separate privacy, store-listing and permission change and is deferred.
3. **No GPS, face or biometric capture**, as the brief requires.
4. **Offline guard entry is deferred.** If added, locally queued entries must be visibly distinct from server-confirmed ones.

## 11. End-to-end tests on a real database

Added 2026-10-28. Run against PostgreSQL 16 with all 21 migrations applied from an empty database.

| Suite | Cases | What it proves |
|---|---|---|
| `tenant-isolation.e2e-spec.ts` | 25 | One society cannot read or change another's data through the API. |
| `visitors.e2e-spec.ts` | 39 | Migration constraints; invitation codes stored only as hashes; scan is atomic (1 of 10 simultaneous scans of a single-use code succeeds; 3 of 12 with a limit of 3); retried requests create one entry; approval is enforced by the server; guards cannot act as a household; committee log scoping; overrides need a reason and are audited. |
| `society-purge.e2e-spec.ts` | 10 | Permanent delete works on a society full of linked billing, accounts, expense, water, visitor and amenity rows (the shape that failed in production); removes users who belonged only to it; keeps a user who also belongs to another society; leaves the other society untouched; writes one audit record with no society attached; refused to a society admin; a wrong confirmation name deletes nothing. |

All 74 pass, repeatedly on the same database (test phone numbers are unique per run). CI runs them in job `api-e2e` (`npm run test:e2e`, serial, `--forceExit`).

**E2E findings**

- **Scan returned "already used or expired" for every valid code** on any server whose database time zone is not UTC. Prisma stores times as UTC in `timestamp` columns, but a JavaScript date passed to raw SQL arrives as `timestamptz` and is compared in the *session* time zone. On the test machine (IST) every window was shifted by 5.5 hours; on a server west of UTC, expired codes would have been accepted. The fake-database unit tests could not see this. Fixed: the statement now compares against the database's own UTC clock (`NOW() AT TIME ZONE 'UTC'`) and binds no date. A unit test pins that no date is bound.
- Two suites reused fixed phone numbers and failed on a second run; both now generate unique numbers per run.

**Still not covered by e2e:** push delivery, email, the mobile app, the web UI (all exercised only by unit tests, render tests or a mock API).

## 13. Phase B — deliveries (added 2026-10-30)

Domestic help, the other half of Phase B, is not started.

**What it does.** A resident tells the gate a delivery is coming and what to do about it: *send them up* (the guard lets the next delivery for that flat in without calling) or *leave it at the gate* (the guard keeps the parcel). The instruction lasts up to 72 hours (12 by default); a "send them up" instruction can allow 1 to 10 deliveries. A guard records a parcel left at the gate, which tells the flat's residents; hands it over (with an optional name of who took it) or sends it back. The committee reads the parcel log on the web.

| Piece | Where | Tests |
|---|---|---|
| Migration `20261030_deliveries` (tables `delivery_expectations`, `parcels`; additive; CHECK constraints on the window and use counts) | `App/prisma/migrations` | applied in `deliveries.e2e-spec.ts` |
| API: resident instructions, parcels, gate routes, committee log | `App/src/deliveries` | `deliveries.service.spec.ts` (service rules, fake DB), `deliveries.http.spec.ts` (who may call what, request shape) |
| A delivery walk-in for a flat with a live "send them up" instruction is approved without asking, and spends one use **in the same transaction** as the entry | `visitors.service.ts` → `consumeAllowEntry` | `deliveries.e2e-spec.ts` (20, real Postgres) |
| Mobile: resident Deliveries screen; guard Parcels tab; warning in the walk-in sheet when the flat asked for "leave at gate"; parcel push opens Deliveries | `Mobile/app/(app)/deliveries`, `Mobile/src/components/gate/ParcelsPanel.tsx` | `deliveries.test.ts`, `deliveries-screen.test.tsx`, `parcels-panel.test.tsx` |
| Web: committee Parcels page (read-only; a parcel at the gate for 3+ days is flagged) | `Web/app/(dashboard)/parcels` | type-check, lint (web has no test runner, see O7) |

**Proved on a real database, which the fake cannot show:** eight deliveries arriving at once for a flat with one use left admit exactly one (7 ask the resident as usual) and the counter reads 1; with three uses, three of five are admitted; a cancelled, expired (judged on the database clock) or other flat's instruction admits nobody; "leave at gate" never admits anyone; only a delivery-type visit consumes an instruction; a retried walk-in does not spend a second use; a hand-over racing a return gives one 200 and one 409; another society's guard gets 404 on our parcels; guards cannot make instructions and residents cannot work the gate.

**Decisions.**
- An instruction is per **flat**, not per company: "send them up" admits the next delivery whichever company it is. The company is shown to the guard but not matched, because guards cannot verify it and a mismatch should not strand a real delivery. A resident who wants it narrower sets one use.
- A "send them up" delivery still shows on the guard's list as *Approved*; the guard presses *Let in*, so entry and exit are logged exactly as for any visitor.
- No photo of the parcel (the camera permission is deliberately not requested; see decision 2 above).

**Not covered.** Push delivery of the new notifications, and the mobile and web screens on a real device or browser, are untested here. Web has no component tests. No reminder is sent for a parcel left uncollected; the committee page only flags it.
